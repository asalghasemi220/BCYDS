Inside a robotics workshop

Can you build a car that stops when its bumper touches a wall?

In a sample activity, students work in pairs to build a model, program a sensor, and test the result. They switch roles and revise their design so each student gets a turn building and coding.

Sample activityPairs · build + code

  1. Build

    One partner assembles the car and attaches the bumper sensor at the front.

  2. Program

    The other partner writes a short program that tells the car what to do when the bumper is pressed.

  3. Test

    Drive it toward a wall. Does it stop in time? Students watch closely and note what happened.

  4. Switch and revise

    Partners trade roles, improve the design, and test again so both get a turn building and coding.

School partnerships

How school partnerships work

  1. Choose a format

    Ask about a single workshop or a series of classes tailored to your students’ ages and learning goals.

  2. Plan the visit

    We’ll agree on group size, dates, classroom setup, and fees with your school. Your school books directly with the society.

  3. Meet the instructor

    Our education partner supplies the instructor and robotics kits. The society coordinates the visit.

Program fees cover delivery costs. Any remaining funds stay with the society to support future youth programs.
