What we offer

Six ways to learn by doing

Robotics is our first program. We’re developing hands-on classes in science, the arts, outdoor learning and everyday life skills, each built around projects students can touch, test and improve.

01/ 06
Robotics
  1. First program

    Robotics

    Students build models, program sensors and test their designs in pairs, switching roles so everyone builds and codes.

    Example projectA car that stops when its bumper touches a wall.
  2. In development

    Science

    Students ask a question, plan a fair test, collect results and explain what they found.

    Example projectWhich materials keep an ice cube frozen the longest?
  3. In development

    Arts

    Students experiment with materials and techniques, create work together and share it with others.

    Example projectA class mural where each pair designs one panel.
  4. Exploring

    Outdoor learning

    Learning moves outside so students can observe, map and investigate the places around their school.

    Example projectMap the living things in your schoolyard.
  5. In development

    Life skills

    Practical skills students use every day, like planning, budgeting and looking after themselves and others.

    Example projectPlan and budget a class event from start to finish.
  6. In development

    Hands-on projects

    Everyday life skills learned by making real things: measuring, building, fixing and finishing a project.

    Example projectDesign and build a planter box for the classroom.
Why hands-on

New opportunities for every student

  • Discover new interests

    Trying something real, with their own hands, shows students what they enjoy and what they’re good at.

  • Solve problems

    Projects rarely work the first time. Students test, find what went wrong and try again.

  • Work together

    Students work in pairs and teams, share roles and learn from each other’s ideas.

Inside a robotics class

Can you build a car that stops when its bumper touches a wall?

In a sample activity, students work in pairs to build a model, program a sensor and test the result. They switch roles and revise their design so each student gets a turn building and coding.

  1. BuildOne partner assembles the car and fits the bumper sensor.
  2. ProgramThe other writes what happens when the bumper is pressed.
  3. TestDrive it at a wall. Does it stop in time?
  4. Switch and reviseTrade roles, improve the design, test again.
School partnerships

How it works

  1. Choose a format

    Ask about a single workshop or a series of classes tailored to your students’ ages and learning goals.

  2. Plan the visit

    We agree on group size, dates, classroom setup and fees with your school. Your school books directly with the society.

  3. Meet the instructor

    Specialist instructors deliver each class with the materials it needs. The society coordinates the visit from start to finish.

Not-for-profit. Program fees cover delivery costs. Any remaining funds stay with the society to support future youth programs.
